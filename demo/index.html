Why was this built?

These are traps built to capture rogue agents — not people. When Tarpit spots a rogue agent, it diverts the agent into a trap instead of the real sign-in. The agent is held there, identified, and removed, and nothing real is inside a trap for it to steal. You can turn each trap on or off.

How Tarpit decides

It gives every visitor an ID code, then asks three simple questions. That is all.

1

Did they follow the normal steps?

A real person opens the login page first, then signs in. An unauthorized AI bot often skips straight to the secret parts. That looks wrong.

2

Do they act like a real person?

People click and type at human speed, in a normal browser. Unauthorized AI bots move too fast, too perfectly, and give themselves away.

3

Are many working together?

One strange login might be a mistake. Hundreds at once, all using the same stolen password, is an attack.

🪤 Trap and capture them

Rogue agents never reach the real sign-in. They are diverted into a trap where they are held, watched, and identified — so they can be removed for good. The trap holds bait only, nothing real.

🧭 See where they came from

Tarpit writes down where the visitor came from, how it got in, and what it was after — so you have a clear record.

✕ Remove them

It does not just hold them, because that would clog things up. It ends their visit, cancels their access, and blocks that ID code if they try again.

Good to know

  • This page is a demo. The visitors are made up. Nothing real is being watched, trapped, or blocked.
  • Start by just watching. In real use, you would watch first and only start stopping visitors once you trust it.
  • Only for your own systems. A real version would only be used on computers you own, or where you have permission.
  • No fighting back. Tarpit only watches, diverts rogue agents into traps, and removes them. Nothing more.
  • The traps hold bait only. No real passwords are ever put in them. The traps exist to capture rogue agents — real people are never sent to one.
